Why Do Octopuses Have Three Hearts and Blue Blood?
Huy (happily): "Riddle time! Who has three hearts, eight arms and blue blood?" Beo (surprised): "A space monster!" Hùng (proudly): "Nope. Meet this little friend. It's an octopus!"
Huy: "Here is a surprise. Octopus blood is not always blue. Sometimes it has almost no colour at all! Today we will find out why an octopus has three hearts, and why its blood turns blue."
Bối (happily): "I know! It has eight arms. So it needs lots of hearts to move them all." Beo (joyfully): "Or three hearts so it can love three times more!" Huy (laughing): "That is the sweetest guess ever, Beo."
Bối (happily): "And its blood is blue because it drinks the blue sea!" Hùng (proudly): "Let's check. Here is a glass of seawater. Look, it's clear, not blue!"
Huy: "Great thinking, both of you. Arms really do need blood. But that's not the whole story. The real story is about something you can't even see. It is called oxygen."
Huy: "Oxygen is an invisible gas. It is in the air, and a little bit is mixed into water too. Every animal needs it all the time to keep its body working. It is a bit like fuel for a car."
Huy: "So how does oxygen get to every part of you? Your blood carries it, like tiny delivery trucks. The blood travels in tubes called blood vessels. And the heart is the pump that pushes it along."
Huy: "Your heart is about the size of your fist. It has two sides. One side sends blood to your lungs to pick up oxygen. The other side sends that fresh blood all around your body."
Beo (surprised): "Like my water squirter?" Huy (happily): "Yes! A heart squeezes and pushes, just like that. Here is our first big idea. A heart is a pump that moves oxygen around the body."
Huy: "Now let's meet the octopus up close. Eight arms with suckers. Two big eyes. A soft body with no bones at all. A hard beak, a bit like a parrot's. And hidden inside are its gills."
Bối (surprised): "Gills? Like a fish?" Huy: "Yes! An octopus has no lungs. Gills are like lungs for living in water. As water flows over them, they pull oxygen out of it."
Huy: "And here comes the surprise. Next to each gill sits a small heart. That makes two gill hearts. Their job is to pump blood through the gills, so the blood can pick up oxygen."
Huy: "Then the blood, full of oxygen, flows to heart number three. This is the body heart. It pumps the blood out to the arms, the brain, and everywhere else."
Bối (happily): "Oh! So our one heart does two jobs, and the octopus has different hearts for different jobs!" Huy (proudly): "Exactly, Bối. You just thought like a scientist."
Hùng (proudly): "I built a model! Squeeze, squeeze for the gills, then the big one for the body. Whoa!" Beo (laughing): "Hùng got a face bath!" Huy (laughing): "Here's takeaway number two. Two hearts pump blood to the gills, and one heart pumps it to the rest of the body."
Bối (surprised): "But why is octopus blood blue?" Huy: "Let's zoom into our own blood first. It is full of tiny red blood cells. Inside them is a helper called hemoglobin. It holds on to oxygen using iron, the same metal that nails are made from. It helps make our blood red."
Huy: "Octopus blood uses a different helper. It is called hemocyanin. Say it with me. He-mo-SY-a-nin. Instead of iron, it holds on to oxygen with copper." Beo (laughing): "Hemo... cyan... achoo!"
Hùng (proudly): "Here's some copper. It's the shiny orange-brown metal you find inside lots of electric wires." Huy: "Each hemocyanin helper has two tiny bits of copper. Together they grab on to oxygen."
Huy: "Now the cool part. When the copper helper is carrying oxygen, it turns blue. When it gives the oxygen away to the body, it loses its colour. That's why octopus blood is not always blue!" Bối (surprised): "It changes colour like a magic trick!"
Huy: "There is one more difference. Our helpers ride inside blood cells, like passengers on a bus. Octopus helpers float freely in the liquid of the blood."
Huy: "Scientists first found this copper helper in the common octopus about 150 years ago. Here's takeaway number three. Octopus blood is blue because it carries oxygen with copper, not iron."
Huy: "So which helper is better? In the same amount of blood, most copper helpers carry only about a quarter as much oxygen as our red helper." Beo (worried): "Poor octopus!"
Bối (surprised): "Then why doesn't an octopus just have red blood?" Huy: "Good question! Scientists think copper blood works well in cold water that has little oxygen. In places like that, the red helper doesn't work as well."
Huy: "And octopuses are not alone. Crabs, some spiders and some scorpions use copper helpers too. So blue blood is not so strange after all!"
Huy: "This is where the three hearts come in. Scientists think the gill hearts give the blood an extra push through the gills. Then the body heart sends it on its way. More pumping may help blood that carries less oxygen keep up. Scientists are still studying how it all works."
Huy (happily): "Here's takeaway number four. Scientists think copper blood works well in the cold sea, and three hearts may help keep it moving."
Huy (happily): "Imagine if you had three hearts like an octopus!" Hùng (laughing): "Boom, boom, boom! Three drums! Oops, I tripped over my own arms." Beo (joyfully): "You need more feet, not more hearts!"
Huy: "You can feel your own heart at home. Sit quietly. Put your hand flat on your chest, a little to the left. Then wait and feel." Bối (surprised): "I can feel it! Thump, thump, thump."
Hùng (tired): "I just hopped ten times. Now my heart is going faster. It's pumping more blood so my body gets more oxygen." Huy: "That's your one heart working hard. An octopus has three pumps sharing the work."
Huy: "So here's the answer. An octopus has two hearts for its gills and one heart for its body. Its blood is blue because it carries oxygen with copper, which scientists think works well in the cold sea."
Huy: "And here's a fun fact. Octopuses have no bones. Their only hard part is the beak. So an octopus can squeeze through a hole about as big as its eye!" Beo (surprised): "Through a hole as small as an eye? Wow!"
Huy (happily): "Next time, we'll find out why octopuses squirt ink! Subscribe to Ask Huy so you don't miss it." Bối (joyfully): "Bye, everyone!"
