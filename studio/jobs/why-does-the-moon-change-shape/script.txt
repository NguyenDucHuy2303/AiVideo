Why Does the Moon Change Shape?
Bối (surprised): "Huy, look! Two weeks ago the Moon was a big round ball. Now it is a thin banana! Did someone take a bite?" Beo (happily): "Maybe a giant ate it for breakfast!" Huy (laughing): "Nobody is eating the Moon. And here is a surprise. The Moon is always a round ball. It never really changes shape at all!"
Huy: "So why does it look so different from night to night? Today we will find out what lights up the Moon, where it travels, and why we see a new shape each night."
Bối (proudly): "I know! The Earth's shadow covers part of the Moon. That makes the dark bit." Huy (happily): "That is a great guess, Bối. Lots of grown-ups think so too. And you are right that the Earth has a shadow."
Beo (happily): "I think the Moon goes to bed. It pulls up a blanket!" Huy (laughing): "A sleepy Moon with a blanket! I love it, Beo. Guessing is how scientists start."
Huy: "Sometimes the Moon really does slide into Earth's shadow. That is called a lunar eclipse. But it can only happen at full Moon, and not every month. So the shadow is not why the Moon changes shape night after night." Bối (surprised): "So what is it then?"
Huy: "Clue number one. The Sun makes its own light. The Moon does not. The Moon is a big ball of rock and dust, with no light of its own." Hùng (proudly): "I brought a Moon rock! Well, a rock from the garden. It does not glow either."
Bối (surprised): "Then why does it shine so brightly at night?" Huy: "Because sunlight lands on it and bounces back to our eyes. It is like a football in a dark room. You only see it when a torch shines on it." Hùng (happily): "Torch off, no ball. Torch on, there it is!"
Huy: "The Moon is round like a ball. So the Sun can only light the half that faces it. That half is bright, like daytime. The other half is dark, like night." Bối (happily): "Just like Earth! Half has day and half has night."
Huy: "Right! The line between the bright half and the dark half has a funny name. It is called the terminator." Beo (surprised): "Terminator? Is it a robot?" Huy (laughing): "No robots, Beo. It just means the edge where day meets night."
Huy: "So here is step one. The Sun always lights up one half of the Moon."
Huy: "Clue number two. The Moon does not stay still. It travels around the Earth in a big loop. This trip is called an orbit."
Huy (happily): "It takes the Moon about 29 and a half days to go through all its shapes and come back to the same one. That is about one month." Bối (surprised): "A whole month for the shapes to repeat?" Huy (happily): "Yes, Bối. The Moon's loop around Earth is enormous, and it takes about a month to go round."
Huy: "Here is something odd. The Moon always shows us the same face. Hùng, walk around Beo and keep looking at him." Beo (joyfully): "I only see your face! Never your back!" Hùng (tired): "Round and round. Whoa, now the whole room is going round!"
Huy (happily): "Exactly. Scientists call this tidal locking. It means the same side of the Moon always faces Earth. The other side is called the far side. We can hardly ever see it from here. The Moon wobbles a tiny bit, but most of the far side stays hidden."
Huy: "Step two. The Moon travels around Earth about once a month, always showing us the same face."
Huy: "Now let us put the clues together. Wherever the Moon goes, the Sun lights the half that faces the Sun. But we are looking from Earth, in the middle."
Huy: "When the Moon is between Earth and the Sun, its bright half faces away from us. We see only the dark half. That is the new Moon, and we can hardly see the Moon at all." Bối (surprised): "The Moon is still there, just hiding in the dark!" Beo (laughing): "Peekaboo, Moon!"
Huy: "About a week later, the Moon has gone a quarter of the way around. Now we see half of its bright half. It looks like half a circle. We call it a half Moon."
Huy: "About a week after that, the Moon is on the opposite side of Earth from the Sun. Now the whole bright half faces us. Full Moon!" Bối (joyfully): "The big round one!"
Bối (worried): "Wait. So the Moon is not really getting smaller?" Huy (firmly): "Not one bit. The Moon is always a whole ball, and half of it is always lit. What changes is how much of that bright half we can see from Earth."
Huy: "Step three. The shape we see depends on where the Moon is in its trip, and how much of its sunny half points at us."
Huy: "Scientists give the shapes names. There are eight main phases. A phase just means the shape the Moon seems to have on that night." Hùng (surprised): "Here is the chart! Oops, it is upside down. There we go."
Huy: "A crescent is a thin curved sliver, like Bối's banana. Gibbous is the word for a Moon that is more than half lit, bulging out like a full tummy." Bối (laughing): "Gibbous! That sounds like a name for a monkey!" Beo (joyfully): "Can I have a pet gibbous?"
Huy: "From new Moon to full Moon, the bright part grows each night. We say the Moon is waxing. From full Moon back to new Moon, it shrinks. We say the Moon is waning."
Huy: "Then the whole pattern starts again, about every 29 and a half days. It is so regular that people all through history have used the Moon to count time, like a giant clock in the sky."
Huy: "Step four. The Moon goes through eight phases, growing and then shrinking, and the pattern repeats about once a month."
Huy: "Want to see the phases yourself? Ask a grown-up to help. You need a torch, a ball and a dark room. Put the torch on a table so it shines toward you. The torch is the Sun." Hùng (happily): "Torch ready, ball ready, curtains closed!"
Huy: "Your head is the Earth. Hold the ball out in front of you, a little above your head. That ball is the Moon. And never stare straight into the torch." Hùng (worried): "Whoops, the Moon slipped!" Beo (proudly): "I caught the Moon!"
Hùng (surprised): "I am turning slowly and the bright part of the ball is changing! Now a thin crescent. Now half. Now it is all bright!" Huy (proudly): "You just made the phases of the Moon. The ball never changed. Only where it was changed."
Huy: "Here is another try. With a grown-up, look at the Moon each night for a month and draw its shape. You will see it wax and wane, just like our chart." Bối (joyfully): "I will draw a Moon every night!"
Huy: "So why does the Moon change shape? The Sun always lights half of the Moon. As the Moon travels around Earth, we see different amounts of that bright half."
Huy: "And here is a fun fact. Some people call the far side the dark side of the Moon. But it is not dark! The Sun shines on it too. At new Moon, when we see no light at all, the far side is all lit up." Bối (surprised): "So the hidden side has its own sunny days!" Beo (happily): "Sunny on the back!"
Huy: "Next time, we will find out why the sky is blue here on Earth but black on the Moon. Subscribe to Ask Huy so you do not miss it!" Bối (joyfully): "Bye! Keep looking up!" Beo (joyfully): "Bye bye, Moon!"
