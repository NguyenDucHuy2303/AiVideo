Chu Dong Tu and Princess Tien Dung
Hello, dear children, and welcome to Huy Tells Tales! Can you imagine being so poor that a father and his son had only one loincloth to share? And yet that boy became an immortal saint! Today, Huy Tells Tales will tell you the legend of Chu Dong Tu and Princess Tien Dung.
Once upon a time, in the days of the eighteenth King Hung, in Chu Xa village by the Red River, there lived a father and his son from the Chu family. The father was named Chu Cu Van, and the son was named Chu Dong Tu. They lived a quiet, kind life in a small house on stilts. Father (happily): "Come, my son, let's go fishing in the river together!" Chu Dong Tu (joyfully): "Yes, Father! Today I will catch lots and lots of fish for you."
One night, the house on stilts suddenly caught fire. The flames burned the roof and all their belongings. Chu Dong Tu (scared): "Father! Come out here quickly!" Father (sadly): "Thank goodness we are both safe. We lost our things, but we can start again, my son."
From then on, the father and son were very poor. They were so poor that the whole family had only one loincloth left. Whoever went out to work wore it, and the other stayed inside the little hut. Chu Dong Tu (happily): "Today you wear the loincloth to the field, Father. I will stay home and weave baskets."
Years went by. The father grew old and weak, and then he became very sick. He called his son close to his side. Father (tired): "My son, I am going far away soon. Please keep the loincloth and wear it." Chu Dong Tu (crying): "Father, please do not worry about me."
When his father passed away, Chu Dong Tu missed him very much. He could not bear to let his father go with nothing to cover him. Chu Dong Tu (firmly): "I would rather suffer myself than let Father be cold and alone." So he wrapped the loincloth around his father and buried him with great care.
From then on, he had nothing to wear. At night, he went to the river to fish. In the daytime, he stood in the water up to his waist and went near the boats to sell fish or ask for food. Chu Dong Tu (happily): "Uncle, would you like to trade for fish? I caught them last night, and they are very fresh." Boatman (laughing): "What a hard-working young man! I will trade you this ball of rice for them."
At that same time, King Hung had a daughter named Tien Dung, who was beautiful and gentle. She was grown up but did not want to marry yet. She only loved to travel by boat and look at the mountains and rivers. Tien Dung (happily): "Father, please let me travel by boat to see our beautiful country." King Hung (firmly): "Go, my child, and be careful. Take your maids and guards with you."
One fine day, the princess's boats floated down the Red River. Bronze drums boomed, flutes played sweetly, and servants bustled about. Maid (joyfully): "Princess, the riverbank is so beautiful!"
Chu Dong Tu was fishing in the river when he heard the drums and flutes. He saw a fleet of big boats coming closer. Chu Dong Tu (scared): "The princess's boats! I have nothing to wear. Where can I hide now?" He hurried up onto the sandy bank, to a clump of reeds, and dug into the sand until he was well hidden.
The boats stopped at the shore. Tien Dung stepped onto the sand to take a walk. She saw the cool green reeds and told her maids to put up a curtain so she could bathe. Tien Dung (happily): "Please put the curtain around these reeds, girls." By chance, that was the very spot where Chu Dong Tu was hiding.
The maids scooped up river water and poured it over the sand. The water flowed and the sand washed away. Suddenly, a young man appeared, lying under the sand. Maid (scared): "Oh! Princess, there is a person under the sand!" Tien Dung (surprised): "Who is there? Why are you lying here?"
Chu Dong Tu trembled, feeling both afraid and shy. Chu Dong Tu (scared): "Please forgive me, Princess! I am so poor and have nothing to wear, so I hid here." Tien Dung (worried): "Do not be afraid. Girls, quickly bring him some clothes to wear."
After he put on the clothes, he told her everything about the fire, the loincloth, and burying his father. Chu Dong Tu (sadly): "My father and I had only one loincloth. When he died, I wrapped it around him." Tien Dung (surprised): "You loved your father that much? What a loving and respectful son you are."
Tien Dung saw that he was loving, honest, handsome, and strong. She thought for a moment and then spoke. Tien Dung (happily): "We met each other by chance like this. Surely heaven has planned it." Chu Dong Tu (surprised): "I am so poor. How could I dare to marry a princess?" Tien Dung (laughing): "Heaven has decided already. Please do not say no."
Right there on the boat, the two held a wedding ceremony. The bronze drums rang out merrily, and everyone was happy. Maid (joyfully): "Congratulations, Princess and Master Chu!"
The news traveled to the capital. When King Hung heard that his daughter had married without asking her father, he was very angry. King Hung (angrily): "Tien Dung married without asking her father. From now on, she may not return to the palace!"
Tien Dung was very sad, but she did not dare to disobey her father. Tien Dung (sadly): "Father is angry. Well, let us stay here, husband and wife, and work hard." Chu Dong Tu (firmly): "Do not worry. I will work very hard with you."
The couple opened a market by the river, called Ha Tham Market. They traded fish, rice, cloth, and clay pots with the villagers. Boats came in crowds. Villagers (happily): "The couple's market is so merry! The trading is fair, and everyone is honest." Everyone loved and respected the two of them.
One day, someone suggested that trading in faraway places would bring great profit. Tien Dung urged her husband to listen. Tien Dung (happily): "Please sail away with the merchant boat, so you can see many more places." Chu Dong Tu (happily): "I will go, and I will come back to you soon."
The merchant boat sailed out to sea and stopped at a mountain called Quynh Vien. Chu Dong Tu climbed the mountain and found a small hermitage. Inside sat an immortal elder with snow-white hair and beard. Immortal Elder (laughing): "You, dutiful young man, I have waited for you for a long time." Chu Dong Tu (surprised): "Venerable sir, how do you know me?"
He gave his goods to the boatman to buy wares, and stayed behind to learn magic. Chu Dong Tu (happily): "Please go and buy goods for me, Uncle. I would like to stay and learn from the elder." Boatman (happily): "All right, I will stop by and pick you up on the way back!"
When the boat came back for him, the immortal elder gave him a staff and a conical hat. Immortal Elder (firmly): "This is a magic staff and a magic hat. Keep them safe and use them only to do good." Chu Dong Tu (joyfully): "Thank you, Elder! I will remember your words."
Back home, he told his wife everything. Tien Dung listened and understood many things. Tien Dung (happily): "Then let us stop trading and go together to find a teacher to learn the Way."
The couple traveled far and wide. One day it grew dark, the road was empty, and there was no house to rest in. Tien Dung (tired): "Husband, my feet are so tired. It is dark, and I cannot see a single house." Chu Dong Tu (happily): "Let us rest here. I will plant the staff and put the hat on top to keep the dew off you."
At midnight, a bright light flashed. Where the staff and hat stood, a whole citadel rose up, with big stilt houses sparkling with gold and jewels. There were servants, guards, and fairy boys and girls too. Tien Dung (surprised): "Husband, wake up and look! It is the elder's magic!"
The next morning, people from all around came, bringing flowers, incense, and sweet fruits. Villagers (joyfully): "Please let us stay here with you both!" From then on, that place became rich, beautiful, and busy, like a little country of its own.
The rumor flew back to the capital. King Hung thought his daughter had built a citadel to fight against him, so he sent soldiers. King Hung (worried): "Tien Dung built her own citadel. Does she want to fight against her father? General Lac, lead the army out at once!" General Lac (firmly): "I obey the king's command!"
The king's army arrived and made camp on the sandbank across the river. The people in the citadel asked to go out and fight. Villagers (worried): "The king's soldiers are here! Please let us go out and guard the citadel!" Tien Dung (firmly): "No. I will never fight against my own father. Let everything happen as heaven wishes."
That night at midnight, a great storm rose with a rumbling roar. Then a dazzling light burst across the sky. The whole citadel, the servants, and the couple, Chu Dong Tu and Tien Dung, floated up into the high heavens. General Lac (surprised): "Oh my! The whole citadel has flown up into the sky!"
In the morning, the old ground had sunk into a huge, wide lake. People called it Nhat Da Lake, meaning the one-night lake. The sandbank where the king's army had camped was called Tu Nhien Bank. When King Hung heard the news, he felt very sorry that he had wrongly doubted his daughter. King Hung (sadly): "I wrongly doubted my child. Oh, Tien Dung, I am so sorry."
Missing the couple, the people built temples in many places. In Gia Lam, Hanoi, there is the Chu Xa communal house, his home village. In Khoai Chau, Hung Yen, there are the Hoa Da Trach Temple and the Da Hoa Temple. Every year in the middle of the second lunar month, a lively festival is held there. Chu Dong Tu is honored as one of the Four Immortals, the four immortal saints of the Vietnamese people, together with Saint Giong, Son Tinh, and Lady Lieu Hanh.
Dear children, Chu Dong Tu was very poor, but he loved his father with all his heart and always lived honestly. That is why everyone loved him, and heaven blessed him.
